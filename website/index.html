Strike your old posts. Keep your password.

sociyx runs on your computer. It scans your own history, shows you the exact items, saves an export, then deletes them slowly and reports what really happened to each one. You log in. You press the button.

pipx install sociyx

Or uv tool install sociyx. Needs Python 3.11 or newer. Try it on fake data first: sociyx tui --demo.

Four steps. Deleting is the last one.

Nothing is deleted by logging in, scanning or planning.

  1. You

    Log in

    A visible browser opens on the platform's own login page. You type your password and pass MFA. sociyx waits.

  2. Read-only

    Scan and filter

    Narrow the list by date range, keyword or content type, and exclude what you want to keep.

  3. You

    Preview and export

    Review the exact items. The plan saves an export first, and deleting needs you to type delete N.

  4. Paced

    Delete slowly

    Paced to stay inside platform limits. Pause, resume after a crash, or cancel. Every item gets its own result.

Results that are not overstated

  • Deleted, verifiedThe delete went through and sociyx checked afterwards that the item is gone.
  • Deleted, unverifiedThe delete was accepted, but the follow-up check could not confirm it.
  • FailedThe delete did not succeed. The report says why.
  • SkippedNot touched, for example because the item changed after you previewed it.

What it won't do. Wipe everything.

It never

  • Bypasses CAPTCHAs, MFA, challenges or rate limits. If one appears, the job stops and waits for you.
  • Types your password for you into a login page.
  • Touches other people's content, or accounts you do not own.
  • Claims your history is completely wiped.

It cannot

  • Reach everything. Platforms do not expose a complete history, so sociyx only acts on what it can list.
  • Delete copies it does not control: other people's quotes and screenshots, caches, archives, federated copies.
  • Undo. The export is a record of what you removed, not a restore.
  • Confirm every delete. Hence verified and unverified.

Instagram carries real account risk. There is no official way to do this, so sociyx uses an unofficial API that conflicts with Instagram's terms. It can trigger challenges, blocks or, in the worst case, a disabled account. Slow pacing lowers the chance but does not remove it. Read the safety page.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. It is MIT-licensed open source. There is no account, subscription or hosted service, and nothing to pay for.

Will it delete everything I have ever posted?

No, and it will never claim to. Platforms do not expose your complete history, so sociyx can only act on what it can list. Reports say what was and was not covered. A finished cleanup is not proof that nothing remains, and copies it does not control (other people's quotes and screenshots, caches, archives, federated copies) stay where they are.

Can I undo a deletion?

No. Before deleting, sociyx writes an export of exactly the items in your plan, so you keep a copy of what you removed, but it does not re-post anything. That is why the final step asks you to type delete and the number of items.

Can my account get restricted?

On Instagram, yes, it is a real risk: sociyx uses an unofficial API, which conflicts with Instagram's terms and can trigger challenges or blocks. Slow pacing lowers the chance but does not remove it. Bluesky and Mastodon use their public APIs. See the safety page.

What about CAPTCHAs and two-factor codes?

You handle them yourself, in a visible browser window or on the platform's own page. sociyx never solves or bypasses a CAPTCHA, MFA prompt or challenge. If one appears mid-run, the job stops and waits for you.

Can it delete other people's content, or someone else's account?

No. It is for your own history only, and you should not run it against accounts you do not own.

Why a terminal app?

It is the simplest way to keep everything on your machine with no server involved. There is a guided terminal UI (run sociyx with no arguments) and plain commands for scripts. A local web UI is planned, but it does not exist yet.

Does it use AI?

No. Filters are plain date, keyword and content-type rules, and you see the exact items before anything is deleted.

Is it the same as Redact.dev?

No. It is inspired by the idea behind Redact.dev but is a separate project. No Redact code, branding or assets are used.
